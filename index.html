What the numbers mean

Overall performance is positive. More people are following our pages, and our content is generating more views and interactions.

Combined views +16.0% YoY
Overall performance

Across all three stores, lifetime post views reached 1.61 million (+16.0%). Interactions increased 14.6% and average views per post increased 16.7%, despite publishing 0.6% fewer posts.

Audience growth

Every store gained more net Facebook followers than last year. Combined net follows increased from 812 to 1,950 (+140%). St. Pete +89%, Tampa +249%, and Lakeland +68%.

Why Tampa's October comparison is different

In October 2025, two viral posts generated 110,005 views together. Those two posts alone had more views than all of June and August 2025 combined (105,477). The larger viral post received over 70,000 views, but resulted in only 2 sales and about $30 in total profit, based on reported sales results.

What if October 2025 had been a very strong month without those exceptional viral results? Tampa's highest other month was February (65,882 views). If October 2025 had performed 20% better than that month (79,058 views), October 2026 would still be up 1.9%, and Tampa's January–October total would be up 11.6% year over year, instead of -4.8%.

One Meta ecosystem, overlapping audiences

Facebook and Instagram are both part of Meta, with significant audience overlap. Many people use both, but may only view or interact with a particular post on one. Our audience can shift between the two depending on how we market our content. For example, our giveaways encourage Facebook interaction rather than Instagram interaction.

Each store tends to have one more consistent platform and another where performance varies more. Which platform performs best differs by store and appears to reflect its customer demographics. We should therefore evaluate combined performance first, while continuing to track each platform separately.

Lakeland's Instagram views fell 44%, but Facebook views increased 90%, resulting in 24% more combined views. The Instagram decline is worth monitoring, but is less concerning while combined performance continues to grow. If Instagram views fall sharply without enough Facebook growth to offset the loss, we should reevaluate our strategy.

The October example is hypothetical. All report totals still include the actual 2025 viral posts and their views.
